## C. Sarung / finger bargaining in Indonesian livestock markets

Verdict: partly documented, but NOT for blantik in Madura/Java in the sources found.

1. Minangkabau "marosok" (goodnewsfromindonesia): https://www.goodnewsfromindonesia.id/2021/10/14/marosok-tradisi-tawar-menawar-ternak-gunakan-bahasa-isyarat-di-minangkabau . Describes livestock bargaining with hands hidden under a sarong, hat or towel; sellers and buyers grip and touch fingers, each finger position signals a price, and the fingers stop when agreed.
2. Journal: Analisis Peran Blantik pada Sistem Pemasaran Domba, Pasar Hewan Tanjungsari: https://jurnal.unigal.ac.id/mimbaragribisnis/article/view/18492 . Documents blantik as intermediary; no sarung/finger detail seen in the snippet.
3. Journal: Analisis peran Blantik sapi, Pasar Sapi Semanding Tuban: https://digilib.uinsa.ac.id/id/eprint/86725/ . Documents blantik facilitating negotiation; no finger/sarung detail seen in the snippet.

Summary: The hidden hand-signal method under cloth is documented for livestock trade in West Sumatra (Minangkabau, "marosok"), with a sourced description of finger touches signaling prices. The searches found no source documenting the practice for blantik in Madura or Java. Sources on Madura cattle markets (Pamekasan, Semanding Tuban) describe the blantik as a negotiating intermediary but do not mention finger or sarung bargaining. The practice's Madura/Java version should be treated as unverified. A general-knowledge claim about "main sarung"/"mbedhung" in Java came from memory, not from a source, and was not confirmed. Do not cite it as fact.
